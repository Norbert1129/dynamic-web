Dynamic 🕸 Journey 1

I decided to move into dynamic web development because I wanted to challenge myself and start understanding more complex systems. Instead of just building static pages, I wanted to see how everything works together behind the scenes.

What I really like about this approach is that I can observe everything in real time. I can follow how request are handled, how the server responds, and how different parts of the application connect. This has helped me a lot in improving my problem-solving skills. I'm getting better at finding errors, reading logs, and understanding what's actually happening when something doesn't work.

At the beginning of every new topic, I actively use tools like AI, Google and YouTube to help me understand how the system works. These resources are very useful for building a solid foundation. Once I feel like I understand the basics, I start experimenting on my own> I intentionally break things and try to fix them without relying on external help. This approach helps me develop a deeper understanding and builds my confidence in solving problems independently.

I chose this way of learning beacuse it keeps me engaged. It is not passive. I'm constantly building, testing, breaking things, and fixing them. Since I want to move towards a full-stack role in the near future, this feels like the right direction. I am working with real-world problems, like figuring out why a page doesn't load, where to look for the issue, and how to fix it.

My goal is to keep going deeper and truly understand how these systems work, not just on the surface but underneath as well. I feel like I'm making steady progress, and that motivates me to keep going.
